# Strider Settgast

My work sits at the intersection of physics, mathematics, and computation.

I'm most interested in problems where the important structure is not directly visible: where you have to reason from measurements, build a model of what might be happening underneath, and then design a way to tell whether that model is actually right. Physics is my main formal anchor, while mathematics and computation are the tools I use to move across problems and fields.

I tend to be drawn to open-ended technical work rather than problems with a prescribed path. That has led me through inverse problems, scientific computing, machine learning, signal analysis, geometry, neuroscience, and quantitative modeling. I care less about treating those as separate identities than about understanding the ideas that recur between them and turning that understanding into experiments, simulations, and working systems.

I use computation heavily as a scientific tool, including modern AI-assisted development workflows. The part I enjoy most is learning unfamiliar material, building a coherent picture of a problem, and then testing that picture against something concrete.
